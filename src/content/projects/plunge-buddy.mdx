## Problem

A cold plunge is only worth the trip when the air, the water, the waves and the tide line up. Those four facts live on three different websites.

The aim was one screen on the phone that answers the question in a glance.

## Options

Three routes were open. The first two cost nothing to build but failed the one-glance test.

The app had to fall back to Vancouver, BC when location permission is refused. There is no account, no analytics and no server of our own.

- **Everything on one screen**: Air, water, waves and tides together, with no scrolling between sources.
- **Uses the current location**: Conditions for where the phone is, with a sensible default when location is refused.
- **No account and no tracking**: Nothing stored about the user beyond preferences kept on the phone.
- **Little to maintain**: No server to run and no data to keep.

| Option | Everything on one screen | Uses the current location | No account and no tracking | Little to maintain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Check three websites | No | Partly | Partly | Yes |
| An iOS Shortcut or widget | Partly | Yes | Yes | Partly |
| **A native SwiftUI app** | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |

Why a native SwiftUI app won: it was the only route that put everything on one screen, used the phone's location, and kept the data on the device.

## Build

Plunge Buddy is an iPhone app written in Swift and SwiftUI with an MVVM structure. Actor-based network services make the API calls thread-safe, with async/await throughout.

It asks OpenWeatherMap for air temperature, Open-Meteo for water temperature and wave height, and WorldTides for the last and next high and low tides, and shows them on one glassmorphic card.

An optional recommendation uses Apple Intelligence on the device, working only from the current conditions and the temperature preferences stored on the phone. Nothing is sent to an outside AI service.

Plunge Buddy was created by Kieran McConnell. Don maintains it and handles the release.

Opening the app gives the four numbers for the nearest water in a few seconds, with a refresh button for a second look.

The [privacy policy](/privacy/plunge-buddy/) is short because there is little to say: location is used while the app is open and never stored, and preferences stay on the device.

## Lessons

Three free data services means three sets of limits and three ways to fail, so each service has its own actor and its own error handling.

Keeping the AI feature on the device avoided a privacy policy section and a server bill in one decision.
